The things you expect from a messenger

All of it works with your Gmail and a nickname.

  • Sent, delivered, readTicks turn blue when your message is read.
  • Typing and last seenSee who is online and who is writing.
  • Replies and reactionsQuote a message or answer with an emoji.
  • Edit and deleteFix a typo or remove a message for everyone.
  • Voice and video callsCall a friend from the chat. Mute, turn the camera off, hang up.
  • GroupsUp to 30 people in one chat.
  • Pin, mute, blockKeep the important chats on top.
  • SearchFind a chat, or a word inside one.
  • Backup and restoreMove your chats to another device.

Three steps

  1. Log inYour Gmail and a nickname of 3 to 20 letters, digits, _ or -. A taken nickname cannot be used with another Gmail.
  2. Add a friendPress + and type their exact nickname.
  3. ChatMessages arrive live while both of you have Nickchat open.

On your Android phone

Nickchat installs like a normal app. It gets its own icon and opens full screen, with no browser bars.

  1. Open this page in ChromeUse the phone you want to install it on.
  2. Tap the menuThe three dots at the top right, then "Install app" or "Add to Home screen".
  3. Open NickchatFind the new icon on your home screen and log in with your Gmail and nickname.

Good to know

Nickchat is simple on purpose. These are the trade-offs.

  • Your chats are saved in the browser or app you use. Use Settings, then Back up chats, before you clear browser data or change device.
  • Messages travel through a free public relay, so do not send anything secret.
  • A nickname belongs to the first Gmail that uses it. Your Gmail is not checked by Google and is stored only as a scrambled code. If you stay away for over 12 hours the lock can expire.
  • If someone is offline for more than about 12 hours, they miss the message.
  • Photos and voice messages are not supported. Calls are one to one and may fail on very strict networks.
